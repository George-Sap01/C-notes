The wandering lantern drifted quietly along the forgotten avenue, where shadows bent themselves into awkward shapes and the pavement whispered about things that had never happened. A bicycle leaned against a wall of crumbling bricks, its spokes humming softly, as though they were keeping secrets from the passing wind. In that peculiar silence, every leaf seemed determined to invent a story, each gust of air weaving tales of imaginary kingdoms, painted oceans, and staircases that spiraled into nowhere. One could almost believe the road was alive, stretching itself lazily, yawning beneath the weight of invisible travelers. The doorways were tall, though no houses claimed them. Curtains swayed inside windows that lacked glass, suggesting families of echoes still lived there, eating meals of sound and drinking cups of dust. The sky overhead resembled an enormous page waiting for handwriting, and the clouds scribbled across it without caring about grammar or consistency. Some of them shaped themselves into creatures with impossible wings; others dissolved into careless smudges, like ink left too long in the rain. Beneath this shifting atmosphere, footsteps appeared without feet to make them. They clicked upon stones, paused, turned, and wandered again, never leading to anything except the certainty that something unseen shared the same pathway. Cats lounged upon rooftops, their eyes glowing like tiny fires, and they seemed to approve of the emptiness. Occasionally, one cat would yawn, and the gesture echoed louder than thunder. A bench sat beneath a crooked lamppost, and though no one occupied the seat, conversations hung in the air above it. Questions floated there, waiting for answers that would never arrive. "Why are the trees bending toward the south?" one asked. Another murmured, "What happens if the clock refuses to count tomorrow?" And the wind, restless and playful, offered no reply except laughter.Deeper in to the avenue, a fountain gushed without water. Its basin was empty, yet droplets of invisible liquid struck the stone with delicate rhythm, creating a melody only the most patient ear could pretend to hear. Birds perched nearby cocked their heads as though following along. The melody rose, sank, spiraled upward, and at last collapsed into silence. For a long while afterward, the silence itself rang. The more one walked, the less certain the direction became. North blended into south; east tripped into west. Street signs carried words that blurred when stared at too long. Sometimes they changed altogether.
